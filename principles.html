Our Principles

How we charge, how we change,
and what we put in writing.

Every creator we have spoken to has been burned by a platform quietly changing its pricing, its terms, or its take rate. Luma House will not be that platform. These are our commitments — written down publicly, before we have the leverage to break them.

How We Charge

Two revenue streams. No hidden fees.

Luma House will always earn money in one of two ways: a modest monthly subscription, or a modest percentage of what you earn — sometimes a combination. We will never charge you on refunds. We will never mark up payment processing. Below is exactly what we will charge, and when.

Right now, in beta — zero.

Founding creators in cohorts one and two pay no subscription and no platform take rate for their first twelve months. Only Stripe's standard payment processing (roughly 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction) applies, and we pass it through at cost.

Written Commitments

Six things we will never do.

Not promises. Terms. These will be enforceable clauses in every creator's contract at public launch.

01

We will never take more than 10% end-to-end once you're at scale.

Once you're earning $500+ per month, our platform fee plus Stripe's payment processing will never exceed 10% of your gross revenue. Below that threshold, our $19 base subscription may briefly exceed 10% — that is the honest cost of professional tooling before you're at scale, not a bug we're hiding. As you grow, our take shrinks, and above $10K/month on Estate our take rate settles at 3% no matter how large you get. This is written into the terms of service, not a hope.

02

We will never charge on your refunds.

If a subscriber refunds, we refund our fee too. Some platforms keep their cut when you refund the customer. That practice is legal, common, and quietly demoralizing. We will not do it.

03

We will never mark up payment processing.

Stripe's fees pass through at cost. If Stripe charges 2.9% + $0.30, that is what appears on your statement. We do not add a hidden margin on top.

04

We will never charge you in a zero-revenue month.

If you earn nothing in a given month, your subscription pauses automatically. Coming back is free. Growing a business has slow seasons; your platform bill should reflect that.

05

We will never sell your subscribers' data.

Your subscribers are your subscribers. We do not sell them to advertisers, offer them to other creators, or use their information for anything beyond running the platform and giving you your analytics.

06

We will never trap your audience.

You can export your subscriber list, your content, and your entire payment history at any time. If you decide to leave Luma House, we help you leave cleanly. That policy will not change.

How We Change

Every platform changes. Here is how ours will.

We will get things wrong. Prices may need to move. Features will evolve. What we promise is not stasis — it is the process by which change happens, and the protections you keep as we grow.

Feature Requests
Every request is publicly tagged in our roadmap as shipped, planned, deferred with reasoning, or declined with reasoning. No black hole.
New Features
Beta creators get 30-day early access. Public creators get transparent release notes. Nothing is quietly rolled out to test on you.
Pricing Changes
90-day advance written notice to every affected creator. Grandfathering of current pricing for at least 12 months from the announcement.
Terms of Service Changes
Material changes are announced in email with a clear diff, not buried in a "we've updated our terms" footer link.
Founding Creators
Cohort-one founding creators are grandfathered into permanent reduced pricing. That commitment survives ownership, funding, or leadership changes.
Product Updates
Monthly public update on what shipped, what didn't, and why. Written by the person building it, not by marketing.

Trust is not a marketing promise. It is a written commitment.

If you are considering Luma House, read this page carefully and hold us to it. If we ever break one of these commitments without an extraordinarily good reason and a public explanation, we will have failed you — and we will have failed the reason we exist.
